Wait — the balloon company sells paper now?

It sells coherence. A guest's eye follows words: the welcome sign at the door, the table number they hunt for, the place card with their name on it. If those are a mix of printable templates and a felt-tip marker, the room reads as assembled. The Paper Trail designs the whole paper suite in your decor palette so the last 10 feet of the party match the first.

Is this print-ready, or just a picture?

Print-ready. The Print sheets button lays out your chosen pieces as a clean sheet — welcome sign, table numbers, place cards, menu, bar sign, hashtag sign, seating board — sized and specified. Isabella prints on 12×18 metallic or matte cardstock and trims to size, which is why the paper math on this page counts real sheets, not vibes.

Why does it show profit? Isn't that for the vendor?

Because signage is where the margin lives — no helium, no delivery risk, just design and paper. Showing the margin isn't a gimmick; it's the reason Isabella can include a full paper suite in a quote for less than a client expects and still make it worth her afternoon. Same reason the site has a Cost Estimator.

How many place cards do I actually need?

One per guest — plus five spares, because someone always brings a plus-one you didn't know about. The tool prices place cards per guest; order a few extra and keep them blank on the corner of the welcome table for the last-minute guest.

What's the difference between a welcome sign and a seating chart?

A welcome sign greets and sets the mood at the door — it's emotion. A seating chart board tells guests where to go — it's logistics. You want both, and you want them in the same palette, because a guest reads them within thirty seconds of each other and will notice if they clash.

Can I reuse this with the decor I already booked?

Yes — pick the palette closest to your balloon work, drop your names in, and the pieces inherit it. Then take the whole list to the Lookbook or straight to Isabella. The paper and the balloons should look like they were designed on the same afternoon — because from here, they were.
