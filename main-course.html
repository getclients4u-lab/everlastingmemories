The Main Course

Party food & catering planner. This site has planned everything around the meal — the balloons, the paper, the drinks, the dessert, the toasts, the photos. Nobody planned the actual food. So here it is: pick the occasion and your stations, set the dietary mix, and get honest portion math (ounces, pounds and the seconds buffer), a real server count, per-guest cost, a live grazing-board preview, and a printable caterer's brief Isabella can hand straight to the kitchen.

Wait — the balloon company feeds people now?

It always did the hard part of a party; it just never had a page for it. A guest walks in, reads the welcome sign, finds their seat, and then the single thing they judge the whole night on is the food. The Main Course plans the meal in the same palette and the same brain as the balloons, so the table looks like it belongs to the room — and nobody runs out.

How do you know how much food to buy?

Two numbers: ounces per guest (set by the station — a grazing board is ~6 oz, a BBQ spread is ~16) and a seconds buffer (default 12%) because the second pass at a grazing table is real and always underestimated. The tool multiplies it out, adds the occasion's appetite factor, and gives you pounds to buy, not vibes.

Why does it make me count gluten-free and vegan plates?

Because "there's a salad" is not a plate, and it's the fastest way to lose a guest's trust. The tool turns your dietary percentages into real counts — N vegan, N gluten-free, N nut-aware — and Isabella makes each one properly, labels it on a card, and hands it over without a scene. The card is the point: a guest with an allergy should never have to play a game of questions at a buffet.

What's the difference between a grazing table and a buffet?

A grazing table is a feast for the eyes: boards, bowls, no heat, no queue — guests pick at it for hours. A hot buffet is a meal: two mains, two sides, chafing dishes and a line. Most parties want a grazing board first, then one hot station once people have had a drink. Pick both here and the tool prices them together.

Do I really need servers?

For a hot station, yes — one per 25 guests keeps a buffet moving and a chafing dish full. Grazing tables need about half that: mostly someone who refills and resets. Corporate parties need more than either, because nobody sits down and everything is eaten standing up, one hand free.

Can I match this to the balloons and paper I already booked?

Yes — choose the palette closest to your decor and the linen under the food echoes the rest of the room, so the table looks like it was designed on the same afternoon as the balloons. Then take the whole plan to Isabella with the Lookbook and the Sip Station, and the food and the drinks arrive as one menu.
