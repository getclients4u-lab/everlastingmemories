Why do party favors matter so much?

Because the favor is the last thing that happens. The party fades into a blur of photos and songs, but the thing in their hand on the ride home is the receipt for the whole night. Isabella has watched five hundred parties end, and the ones people rave about aren't the most expensive — they're the ones where every single guest left with something that said “we're glad you came.” It costs a few dollars and it buys a feeling that lasts on the kitchen windowsill for a year.

How many favors do I actually need?

One per guest, plus a 10% buffer — Isabella never skips it, because someone always brings a plus-one, a cousin, or a toddler who wants two. For a favor bar with several options, that buffer is applied to each type, so you're never short on the popular one. The Favor Math panel does the arithmetic the second you move the guest slider.

What's the cheapest favor that still feels special?

A personalized tag. At about a dollar and change, a custom tag on any favor turns “here's some candy” into “this was made for you.” It's the cheapest line on the whole bar and the one guests actually keep. Pair it with a bubble bottle or a glow stick and you've spent under two dollars to make someone smile in their car.

Balloons as favors — won't they pop or float away?

A mini balloon buddy is a single inflated latex mini on a stick, tied with your tag — it doesn't float away because it's on a stick, and it lives for days, not minutes. Isabella uses them as the signature favor because they're the one favor that walks out the door at eye level: every other guest sees it leaving and wants one too. Hand a kid a mini balloon at the door and you've ended the party on the highest possible note.

Do favors work for a corporate or office party?

They're quietly the best part of one. A desk-sized favor — a mini succulent, a cookie bag with a printed tag, a seed packet — turns an office gathering into something people keep on their desk and look at. Skip anything that needs explaining and skip anything that crinkles; a quiet, useful, name-tagged favor reads far more premium than another branded pen. The Launch Pad runs the corporate side; the Favor Bar dresses the exit.

Kid party vs grown-up party — does the bar change?

Completely. Kids want volume and instant joy — bubbles, glow sticks, candy bags, mini balloons, and a tag with their name on it. Grown-ups want something that survives the car ride and the week after: seed packets, mini succulents, a really good cookie. Isabella's rule: kid parties split the budget across more, smaller favors; grown-up parties give one beautiful thing. If you're planning a baby shower or first birthday, the Nest and the Smash Hit handle the moment — the Favor Bar handles the goodbye.

Can Isabella build the favors for me, or is this DIY?

Both. Print the tag sheet and this becomes a lovely afternoon with a paper trimmer and a ribbon spool. Or send the brief through the form and Isabella assembles the whole bar — tags printed and cut, bags filled, minis inflated the morning of — so all you do is set them by the door. Most clients do the second one, and every one of them says the same thing at drop-off: “I didn't realize how much of the party this was.”
